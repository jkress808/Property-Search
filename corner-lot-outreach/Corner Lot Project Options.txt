Option 1 — Represent the seller. Sign a listing agreement with the lot owner, market to your builder network, collect the listing-side commission on the land, and have pre-negotiated listing commitments from the builders you sell to (documented in a separate builder-brokerage agreement that says "if we bring you a lot, you give us the exclusive on the vertical product"). Seller is represented throughout. No assignment, no principal conflict. Cleanest path.
Option 2 — Represent the builder. Sign buyer agency with 3–5 builders who each sign a master agreement giving your team the exclusive listing rights on any vertical product arising from lots you source. You cold-outreach owners as the builder's agent, builder makes the offer directly, you collect buyer-side commission at close and listing commission on the new units. Seller is unrepresented but knows exactly who you work for. No assignment in the chain.
Path C — Option agreements with a developer partner. Instead of assigning a PSA, the builder pays the seller a non-refundable option fee (real money, maybe $2–10K) for 30–90 days to do feasibility — title, soils, zoning, survey — and then either exercises or walks. You write it as the buyer's agent for the builder. The seller gets paid something upfront regardless, which is much easier to defend than a wholesaler-style tie-up. This is what sophisticated infill builders already do. You could productize the outreach and be the team that brings them sites.
Path D — The assignable PSA (what you asked about). If you truly want to pursue this, the minimum guardrails are: (1) your firm's designated broker fully read in and approving the playbook, (2) written disclosure to the seller that you are a licensed broker acting as principal and intend to assign for profit, (3) recommend in writing the seller retain their own broker or attorney, (4) separate the assignment fee from any real estate commission and disclose the exact dollar amount to all parties before signing, (5) reasonable earnest money that's actually at risk if you can't assign, (6) no intentionally-opaque entity names designed to hide that licensees are behind it, (7) E&O carrier notified and confirmed coverage. Even with all of that, you're creating fact patterns a DOL investigator or plaintiff's attorney can make look predatory.
