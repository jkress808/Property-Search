00Overview & Economics

The CSV is a list of corner-lot owners in Seattle/Shoreline with assessed value at or below $600K — i.e., where the dirt is worth more than the house. Our role is to be the owner’s listing broker, sell the raw lot to a developer in our network, and secure the exclusive listing on the new construction as a written condition of the introduction.

The two commissions on every deal

LegCounterpartyTriggerTypical take
1. Land sale listing Owner (seller) Close of land to developer 2.5–3% of land price
2. New-construction listings Developer Sale of each finished home/townhome 2.5–3% per unit × N units
Why leg 2 dominates the math A $550K lot that becomes 4 townhomes at $900K each = $3.6M of retail sell-through. At 2.5% that’s $90K on the back end, compared to ~$15K on the front-end land sale. This is why we give up the buyer-side commission and source owners aggressively — we’re underwriting to the finished-home payoff, not the lot trade.

Posture and agency

  • We represent the owner / seller. We are not the developer’s broker on the land purchase.
  • Our compensation on the new-construction listings is a separate written agreement with the developer, executed before the land sale closes.
  • This keeps our fiduciary duty clean: we owe the seller our best effort to maximize their price; the back-end commission is paid by the developer and does not come out of the seller’s proceeds.

01Legal Framework (Washington State)

Because we’re licensed brokers, the usual wholesaler-adjacent exposures (RCW 18.85 unlicensed-brokerage risk, assignment-disclosure gray area) do not apply. The exposures that do apply are the ones every WA listing broker carries — agency relationships, written compensation agreements, and the 2024 NAR-settlement changes to commission disclosure.

RCW 18.86 — Brokerage Relationships Act

  • Deliver the “Real Estate Brokerage in Washington” pamphlet to every buyer/seller we interact with before substantive discussions. This is mandatory under RCW 18.86.030.
  • Execute a written listing agreement (exclusive right-to-sell preferred) with the owner before marketing the lot. Verbal listings are not actionable for commission in WA.
  • If a developer we’ve teed up also wants us as their buyer’s broker, it’s limited dual agency — requires informed written consent from both sides and a modified duty of care. Default to avoiding this on the land purchase; keep it clean.

Written commission agreement — the key document

WA courts have repeatedly held that a broker cannot recover commission without a written agreement signed by the party they expect to be paid by. For this play, that means two signed documents per deal:

  1. Exclusive listing agreement (owner → us) for the land sale.
  2. Future listing commitment (developer → us) for the new-construction units, signed before we introduce the developer to the property. This is the leverage that keeps the developer honest after close.

NAR settlement (effective August 2024)

  • Buyer-broker compensation can no longer be offered through the MLS.
  • Any buyer-broker compensation must be in a written buyer-broker agreement between the developer and their own broker (if any). Since we are not representing the developer, this mostly doesn’t touch us — but the listing agreement with the owner should explicitly state whether any buyer-side compensation is being offered and who pays.
  • The cleanest structure: owner pays only our listing-side commission (2.5–3%) on the land sale. Developer brings their own representation or comes direct. No cooperating broker fee on MLS.
RCW 61.34 — Equity Skimming / Distressed Homeowner Still applies. If any owner on the target list is in default, tax-delinquent, or pre-foreclosure, follow the Distressed Property Law pathway specifically — separate script, separate disclosures. Licensed status does not insulate you from this statute.
RCW 19.86 — Consumer Protection Act Still applies. Don’t understate land value to the owner to push them to sell fast, and don’t overstate development potential to the developer. Both are CPA violations with treble damages and fees.
Firm / broker paperwork Every template below (listing agreement, commission commitment letter, postcard copy) should be reviewed once by your designated broker and your firm’s attorney. After that the forms are reusable across the entire campaign.

What we bring to the table

  1. Side-by-side net sheet. Two columns: (a) traditional as-is home sale net-to-seller, (b) land sale to developer net-to-seller. For most target lots the land path is $100K–$400K higher.
  2. Comparable land sales within 0.5 miles, last 12 months. Pulled from MLS + NWMLS vacant-land data + CoStar if available.
  3. Development scenarios. Under current zoning, what can be built here? (Example: “Your 9,200 sq ft LR2 lot supports 4 townhomes at ~$900K each retail.”)
  4. Developer interest indicator. “We have [N] developers in our network actively buying in [neighborhood] this quarter.” (Truthful — don’t promise a named buyer you haven’t committed.)
  5. Mandatory disclosures. Agency pamphlet delivered and acknowledged, dual-agency possibility explained if applicable, disclosed compensation on the back end.

The listing agreement — specific to this play

  • Exclusive right to sell, 90–180 day term. Avoid open listings; they lose deals to other brokers.
  • 2.5–3% commission to listing firm, no cooperating-broker split (developer brings their own representation or comes unrepresented).
  • Marketing strategy paragraph naming private-network / off-market marketing to developers as the primary channel. Seller consents in writing to a delayed or waived MLS entry.
  • Disclosure of our back-end arrangement. Plain-language paragraph: “Broker discloses that broker may receive additional compensation from the ultimate buyer (developer) as the listing broker on any new construction built on this lot. This additional compensation is paid by the developer, not the seller, and is separate from this agreement.”
  • No dual agency on the land sale without an explicit written consent rider.

Sample owner talking-point pack (for the living-room meeting)

"Mr./Ms. [Owner], Your corner lot at [address] is currently assessed at $[X]. Based on the zoning here — [LR2 / R4 / etc.] — and what we’ve seen local builders pay for similar lots in the last 12 months, we believe a builder would pay between $[low] and $[high]. If you were to sell the house traditionally, after a few repairs, 6% total commission, and closing costs, you’d likely net $[Y]. If we list it as a development lot to our builder network, after our 3% listing fee and closing costs, you’d likely net $[Z], which is about $[delta] more. We’d like to list your lot for 90 days. We market it privately first, to four or five developers we already know are buying. If it doesn’t move, we can re-strategize or terminate at day 90 — your choice. A few things I have to disclose up front: [hand them the agency pamphlet]. Also, our compensation on the land sale is 3%, paid by you. Separately, we have an arrangement with the developer who ends up buying that lets us list the homes they build here. That part is paid by them, not you — but I want you to know about it. Any questions?"
Why written disclosure of the back-end protects us If the owner later discovers we collected a second commission from the developer and we never said so, they can (and will) argue breach of fiduciary duty. Disclosing it in writing inside the listing agreement kills that claim and costs us literally nothing — almost every owner will sign anyway because it’s not coming out of their pocket.

COther Owner-Side Paths

C1. Door-knock campaign

  • Pair with the Path A postcard — knock the top 100 absentee-flagged lots 10–14 days after the mailing.
  • Leave a branded folder with the net-sheet template + agency pamphlet + your card.
  • Conversion 3–8% vs 1–3% for postcard only.

C2. Hand-written letter + comparable sales packet

  • More personal than a postcard; competes less with junk mail.
  • Works especially well on long-time owner-occupants where we need to build trust before they talk about selling.

C3. Facebook + Nextdoor geo-targeted ads

  • Radius target the neighborhoods where the CSV is concentrated.
  • Creative: “See what a builder would pay for your corner lot — free valuation from a local broker.”
  • Drives to the same landing page. Augment, don’t replace, direct mail.

C4. Referral partnerships with local estate attorneys / CPAs

  • Attorneys doing estate planning, trust admin, and probate for long-time Seattle/Shoreline homeowners are sitting on a goldmine of future-listing leads.
  • Offer a written referral fee or co-marketed valuation service.

C5. Net-sheet direct mail (premium version of Path A)

  • Instead of a postcard, mail a personalized 2-page net-sheet (traditional sale vs. developer sale) with the owner’s actual parcel number and zoning printed on it.
  • Cost: $2–$4 per piece. Response rate: 3–5%.
  • The copy quite literally tells them “here is how much more money you’d make if you sold as a lot.” Very persuasive; modest volume.

C6. Dual-listing flip (owner won’t sell as lot)

  • Some owners will refuse to list as a development lot (emotional attachment, “I don’t want my house torn down”).
  • Fallback: offer to list the home traditionally. You keep the listing either way. Don’t walk away from a 2.5% commission because the land play didn’t land.

C7. Pre-listing option from developer

  • For premium lots only: developer pays the owner a small option fee ($2K–$10K) to hold the price for 90 days while permits are scoped.
  • You broker the option; commission earned at exercise.
  • Owners love this because they get cash now with no obligation to do anything.

★Developer Sprint — 3 to 5 Day Playbook

This is the new core. You already have developer relationships and a target list of corner lots. The sprint converts both into written back-end listing commitments and a warm buyer bench — in days, not weeks — so when an owner says yes, you have buyers ready and the back-end commission already locked in paper.

Goal of the sprint Walk out of Day 5 with: (a) 3–5 signed Future Listing Commitment letters from developers covering your top 10–20 CSV lots, (b) a ranked buyer bench per sub-neighborhood, (c) a deal package you can send to a new developer in under 10 minutes.

Day 0 — Prep (half-day, before you talk to anyone)

Build the inputs

  • 0.5h Rank the CSV. Top 20 lots by: lot size ≥ 7,000 sq ft, upzoned (LR1/LR2/LR3 or equivalent), corner geometry confirmed, assessed ≤ $600K, not in environmental critical area.
  • 1.5h For each of the top 20, build a one-page deal sheet (template below) — address, PIN, lot size, zoning, comps, build scenario, owner-contact status.
  • 1h Pull recent vacant-land + teardown sales within 0.5 mi of each lot from NWMLS / CoStar / King County Real Property Sales. Save as comps per lot.
  • 1h Draft & attorney-review the Future Listing Commitment letter template (see section below). One-time task.
  • 0.5h Short-list 15–25 developers: prioritize those who closed infill lots in Seattle / Shoreline / Kenmore / Lake Forest Park in the last 12 months. (King County sales data + NWMLS.)
  • 0.5h Build a shared Google Sheet tracker: rows = developers, columns = lots × status (sent / opened / interested / pass / committed).

Day 1 — Blast + triage

Go wide, fast

  • 08:00 Send personalized emails to all 15–25 developers. Subject line: “Off-market corner lots, Seattle/Shoreline — your buy box”.
  • 08:30 Attach a redacted master PDF (no addresses visible without a return email) and the top 3 deal sheets un-redacted for your tightest relationships.
  • 10:00–13:00 Call the top 8 developers. Voicemail script prepared.
  • 14:00 Schedule 20-minute Zoom or in-person meetings for Day 2 and Day 3 with any developer who expresses interest.
  • 17:00 End-of-day standup with the team: update tracker, identify any lots that already have multiple developers interested (price-tension opportunity).

Day 2–3 — Meetings + commitment letters

Convert interest into signatures

  • 20-minute meetings. Agenda: (1) walk the specific lots they’re interested in, (2) confirm buy box (lot size, price ceiling, zoning, timeline), (3) lay out commission terms on new construction, (4) ask for signed commitment letter today.
  • Commitment letter terms: 2.5–3% per sold unit, exclusive 6-month listing commencing at Temporary Certificate of Occupancy, binding on any land they acquire via our introduction.
  • Developers who won’t sign a commitment letter don’t get the addresses. Period. This is the only enforcement mechanism you have and it works.
  • Field trip option: drive 2–3 of the most interested developers past the top lots on the afternoon of Day 2 or 3. A physical walk-by converts faster than a pdf.
  • For any developer committing to multiple lots, offer a modest bulk discount on the listing commission (e.g., 2.5% instead of 3%) in exchange for first-look rights on every deal that week.

Day 4 — Match + approach owners

Now the owners get the call

  • Match each top-20 lot to the 1–2 developers who committed and fit best.
  • For lots with 2+ developer commitments: prime candidates for a private bidding process. Tell the owner up-front, in the listing conversation, that you already have multiple interested buyers.
  • Send the Path A postcard to the remaining owners who weren’t matched yet — keeps the pipeline filling.
  • For matched lots, skip Path A and go straight to hand-delivered letter + follow-up phone call: “I have a buyer for your lot. I’d like 20 minutes of your time this week.”

Day 5 — Listing agreements

Sign up what you can

  • Sit-down meetings with responsive owners. Path B playbook applies — net sheet, comps, development scenario, pamphlet, written back-end disclosure.
  • Goal: 2–4 signed exclusive listing agreements by end of Day 5 from the top-matched lots.
  • For each signed listing: notify the committed developer the next morning. Private offer window of 5–7 days before MLS (if MLS at all).

02Future Listing Commitment Letter

This is the document that keeps the developer honest after close. It’s a short, standalone letter executed before you introduce the property — not buried in a purchase contract. One page, signed by the developer’s principal, witnessed.

FUTURE LISTING COMMITMENT — [PROPERTY / LOT REFERENCE] This Commitment is made [DATE] between: BROKER: [Firm Name], [Designated Broker], WA License #[#] Address, phone, email DEVELOPER: [Legal entity name], by [Principal Name], Title Address, phone, email 1. INTRODUCTION. Broker has identified and is prepared to introduce Developer to the following real property (the "Property") on which Developer may, at its sole discretion, make an offer to purchase: Address: [Street, City, WA ZIP] Parcel (PIN):[##########] 2. LISTING COMMITMENT. In consideration of Broker's introduction of the Property and of Broker's services in facilitating access to the seller, Developer agrees that if Developer (directly or via any affiliate, parent, subsidiary, nominee, or assignee) acquires the Property within twelve (12) months of the date of this letter, Developer will grant Broker the EXCLUSIVE RIGHT TO MARKET AND SELL each residential unit subsequently constructed on the Property, on the following terms: a. COMMISSION: [2.5% or 3.0%] of the gross sale price of each unit, paid at closing of each unit, from Developer’s proceeds. b. LISTING TERM: Six (6) months per unit, beginning on the earlier of Temporary Certificate of Occupancy or the date each unit is first offered for sale. c. FORM: Customary NWMLS Exclusive Right to Sell listing agreement, executed by Developer within ten (10) business days of TCO or first marketing date. d. PROTECTION: If Developer terminates or withholds the listing and lists through another broker, or sells the unit off-market, Developer owes Broker the full commission set forth above as liquidated damages on each such unit. 3. NO PURCHASE OBLIGATION. This Commitment does not obligate Developer to purchase the Property or make any offer. Developer's decision to pursue the Property is at its sole discretion. 4. BROKER INDEPENDENCE. Broker represents the seller of the Property, not Developer. Developer acknowledges receipt of the "Real Estate Brokerage in Washington" pamphlet. This Commitment does not create an agency relationship between Broker and Developer on the land purchase. 5. CONFIDENTIALITY. Developer agrees to keep all non-public information about the Property and the seller confidential, and not to contact the seller directly other than through Broker, for twelve (12) months from the date of this letter. 6. GOVERNING LAW. Washington State. Prevailing party is entitled to fees. AGREED: __________________________ __________________________ [Designated Broker] [Developer Principal] [Firm Name] [Developer Entity] Date: Date:
Why each paragraph is there
  • § 2(d) “protection clause” closes the end-run where a developer quietly uses another broker after closing. Without it, your commitment is unenforceable in practice.
  • § 3 keeps the letter non-binding on the land purchase itself — makes it palatable to any developer.
  • § 5 is your moat. If the developer backs out and tries to approach the owner directly, this clause converts the behavior into a lawsuit.
  • § 2(b) term starts at TCO, not signing. Crucial because builds take 12–24 months and most listing agreements have stale-date problems if you start the clock too early.

04Seller Clarity & Agency Rules

Because we’re licensed, the clarity bar is actually stricter than a wholesaler’s — we have fiduciary duties. Every owner interaction needs these in place.

Every owner, every time

  1. Agency pamphlet (“Real Estate Brokerage in Washington”) delivered and acknowledged — before any substantive conversation about price, value, or whether they should sell.
  2. Written listing agreement before marketing or contacting any developer about the specific lot.
  3. Written back-end disclosure inside the listing agreement: we may earn a separate commission from the developer on the new-construction listings, paid by the developer.
  4. Net sheet showing both the traditional home-sale path and the land-sale path. Signed by the owner acknowledging receipt.
  5. No verbal promises about price. Give a range, in writing, supported by comps. “I think I can get you $X” verbally → breach of fiduciary duty when we close at $X−10%.

Drop-in disclosure paragraph for the listing agreement

ADDITIONAL BROKER COMPENSATION DISCLOSURE Seller acknowledges and agrees that Broker may, separately and in addition to the commission set forth in this Listing Agreement, enter into a listing agreement with the ultimate buyer of the Property (expected to be a residential developer or builder) for the sale of new-construction residential units subsequently built on the Property. Any such additional compensation will be paid by the developer, not by Seller, and will not reduce the proceeds of this sale to Seller. Seller consents to this arrangement and acknowledges that Broker has disclosed it in writing prior to Seller signing this Listing Agreement. Seller initials: ______ Date: ______
Why this disclosure is self-protecting Without it, the owner can later argue we steered the lot toward a specific developer to secure our back-end commission, instead of maximizing their price. With it signed, that argument collapses — they knew, in writing, before they hired us.

05Liability Guardrails

Licensed-broker risks are different from wholesaler risks. The big three here are fiduciary breach, RCW 18.86 procedural failures, and misrepresentation to either side.

Always do

  • Deliver the agency pamphlet on first substantive contact. Document delivery.
  • Sign a written listing agreement before any developer knows the address.
  • Include the back-end compensation disclosure paragraph in every listing agreement.
  • Sign a Future Listing Commitment letter with every developer before sharing an address.
  • Require NDAs with developers receiving more than one lot’s un-redacted details.
  • Keep written comps backing every price range you state to an owner — saves you on every breach claim.
  • Route all earnest money and proceeds through a neutral title/escrow company. Never touch client funds.
  • Log every contact in the firm’s CRM — with timestamp, pamphlet-delivered-yes/no, and consent status.
  • Keep designated-broker oversight on every listing agreement and commitment letter.

Never do

  • Never share a specific address with a developer before they’ve signed a commitment letter.
  • Never promise a specific price to the owner verbally. Range + comps only, in writing.
  • Never take a listing without delivering the pamphlet first. One procedural misstep can void the agreement.
  • Never enter dual agency on the land sale without the explicit written consent rider.
  • Never market a lot publicly before the listing agreement is fully executed.
  • Never tell a developer a lot is “locked” before you have a signed exclusive listing.
  • Never pressure owners in the “distressed” category (tax lien, pre-foreclosure) without separately complying with RCW 61.34.
  • Never suggest you are the developer’s broker on the purchase if you’re not. Referral ≠ representation.
Recommended stack Designated broker review of all templates → E&O policy in force → firm attorney engagement (one-time template fee) → title/escrow relationship → CRM with audit trail → developer NDA on file for every active contact.

06Recommended Sequence

Given the new model, developer-first is the right sequence. Locking the back-end commission is what makes the economics work — and lining up committed buyers makes the owner pitch materially stronger.

Step 1 — Run the Developer Sprint this week

  • Days 0–3 of the sprint. Walk out with 3–5 signed Future Listing Commitment letters on your top 10–20 lots.
  • This is the single most leveraged thing on the page. Without it, Path A/B drive listings that yield only the front-end commission.

Step 2 — Direct outreach on matched lots (Days 4–14)

  • For each lot with a developer commitment, skip the generic postcard.
  • Hand-deliver or certified-mail a personalized letter: net sheet, comps, and a line you can actually say: “We have a buyer for your lot.”
  • Target 2–4 signed listing agreements in weeks 2–3.

Step 3 — Path A postcard to the rest (parallel)

  • Postcard the remaining corner-lot owners at volume (rest of CSV, ~2,000 pieces).
  • Second touch 3 weeks later.
  • Feed inbound leads into Path B consultations; new committed lots get pulled back into the Sprint workflow for developer match.

Step 4 — Permanent developer program

  • Turn the Sprint into a standing program: every new lot listed goes out to the committed developer list within 24 hours, under confidentiality.
  • Quarterly “lot drop” with your developer network — becomes a channel in itself.
  • Add new developers to the program only after they sign the Future Listing Commitment template for a specific lot.
Why developer-first beats owner-first With written commitments in hand, you walk into every owner meeting with “I have buyers ready.” That converts listings at roughly double the rate of a cold valuation pitch, and guarantees the back-end revenue before you spend a penny on postcards.
